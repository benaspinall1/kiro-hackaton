# The Stories Behind the Shield

---

## Maya — The Accident

11 PM. She's been job hunting for three weeks and she's exhausted. She's been pasting her resume into ChatGPT to polish it — reword a bullet point here, tighten the summary there. She's done it a dozen times.

What she never noticed: an SSN sitting at the bottom. A leftover from a government application template she'd never cleaned up.

She hit send.

The extension caught it. *"This message contains a Social Security Number. Want to remove it before sending?"*

She stopped. Stared. Exhaled.

She clicked redact, saw the before and after, and went to bed safe.

She wasn't careless. She just didn't know it was there.

---

## Priya — The Crisis

2 AM. ER waiting room. Her mother had been rushed in an hour ago and Priya is sitting under fluorescent lights trying to figure out if the secondary insurance covers the imaging they just ordered.

She opens Claude and uploads everything she has — an insurance card photo, a hospital summary, a prescription list. She needs answers and she needs them now.

She is not thinking about data privacy. She is thinking about her mother.

The extension flags it quietly. Medicare ID. Date of birth. Home address.

*"This document contains personal health information. You can redact it and still get the help you need."*

Fifteen seconds. Done. She got her answer.

Her mother's identity stayed hers — even on the worst night of the year.

## James — The Blind Spot

James is writing a paper on financial fraud. He's been collecting case studies for weeks — articles, data, notes from interviews. He pastes a big block of his research into an AI chat to get help structuring the argument.

Buried somewhere in that block: a real person's name, phone number, and address from an interview he'd done months ago. He'd completely forgotten it was even in there.

*"Heads up — this looks like it contains someone's contact information. Want to remove it?"*

He scrolled back. Found it. Felt a little embarrassed. Redacted it and moved on.

The paper got an A. The person he interviewed never ended up in a training dataset.

## The Pattern

These aren't edge cases. They're Tuesday afternoons.

Maya isn't stupid — she's tired and job hunting. Priya isn't careless — she's scared and it's 2 AM. James isn't negligent — he just had too many tabs open and too much on his mind.

These are smart, capable people doing something completely reasonable: using a tool that helps them. The problem isn't the person. It's that the risk is invisible until it isn't.

That's the gap we sit in. One small moment of awareness — quiet, respectful, and fast enough that it doesn't break your flow.

That's it. That's the whole idea.
